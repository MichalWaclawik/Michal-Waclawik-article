Deriving m = (GπT) / c² from Energy Equivalence

Abstract

This paper introduces a speculative theoretical framework linking inertial mass to gravitational coupling, geometric constants and oscillatory time scales. Starting from Einstein’s energy equivalence E = mc² and proposing an alternative expression E = GπT, where T denotes the oscillation period of a fundamental vibrational mode, we derive a mass–frequency relation m = Gπ / (c² f). The model implies that mass decreases with increasing oscillation frequency and approaches zero as f → ∞. Conceptual parallels are drawn with quantum field theory, the Higgs mechanism and string‑theoretic interpretations of mass as vibrational energy.

1. Introduction

The physical origin of mass remains one of the most fundamental open questions in modern theoretical physics. In quantum field theory (QFT), mass arises from interactions with the Higgs field. In general relativity (GR), mass is a source of spacetime curvature. In string theory, mass corresponds to vibrational modes of extended objects. This work proposes a symbolic relation E = GπT, interpreted as a gravitational–geometric energy expression, and derives a new mass–frequency relation by equating it with Einstein’s energy equivalence.

2. Mathematical Framework

We begin with Einstein’s relation: 

E = mc²

We introduce the speculative expression: 

E = GπT

Equating both: 

mc² = GπT

Solving for mass: 

m = (GπT) / c²

Since T = 1/f, we obtain: 

m = Gπ / (c² f)

3. Physical Interpretation

The derived relation implies that high-frequency oscillations correspond to low mass, while low-frequency oscillations correspond to high mass. Massless states correspond to f → ∞. This aligns with the Planck relation E = hf, string theory’s vibrational mass spectrum, and QFT’s interpretation of particles as field excitations.

4. Relation to Existing Theories

The model conceptually parallels the Higgs mechanism, general relativity’s geometric interpretation of mass, and string theory’s vibrational modes. The presence of G and π suggests a geometric–gravitational origin of mass.

5. Predictions and Implications

The model suggests:

  • Massless states correspond to infinite-frequency oscillations.
  • Massive states correspond to slower oscillations.
  • Mass may be a geometric resonance phenomenon.
  • Gravity and geometry may determine vibrational spectra.

6. Limitations

The model is speculative and lacks empirical validation, a Lagrangian formulation, and integration with the Standard Model. Nevertheless, it offers a conceptual bridge between gravitational and oscillatory descriptions of mass.

7. Conclusion

We propose a gravitational–geometric oscillation model of mass, leading to the relation m = Gπ / (c² f). This connects mass to oscillatory frequency, gravitational coupling and geometric constants, and may offer a new perspective on the origin of mass.
